Felis Catus Press is an independent traditional publishing firm in Nigeria, established in 2024. It is an imprint of Nimble House, a creative firm rendering writing and publishing services.

Felis Catus is the scientific name for cats, the only animals that pay the toll fee when descending from heaven. Our wish is therefore to publish poetry that exudes the alertness and quickness of a cat's motion, the sharpness of their eyes, and the emotion of their intimacy.

Through our authors, we seek to teach you something you didn't know, something new and novel, but original and crude in its uniqueness.

We are dedicated to the publication and promotion of poetry written in the finest of craft, poetry enriched with natural and cultural condiments. To us, poetry includes mainstream poems and traditional haibun.

Three steps.

  1. Prepare

    Your poems only, with the title on the first page and no personal information. Garamond, 12.5 pt, single-spaced, saved as a Word document.

  2. Email

    Send it to feliscatuspress@gmail.com. Subject: your name and manuscript title. In the email, give your contact details and list which poems were published before, and where.

  3. Hear back

    We read every manuscript sent in March and announce accepted collections in August.

What we look for.

Your history and heritage: personal, yet specific and generic. Poetry with the alertness of a cat's motion, the sharpness of their eyes, the emotion of their intimacy.

No set theme. Just quality poems in a collection that is coherent, concise and clear.

Who can submit
  • Nigerians, or poets of any nationality resident in Nigeria.

Simultaneous submissions & resubmitting
  • Simultaneous submissions are welcome. If your manuscript is accepted elsewhere, tell us promptly in the same email thread.

If we publish you
  • 10 free author copies; further copies at 35% off.
  • A print run of 100–150 copies.
  • Royalties after production costs: 30% to you, 70% to the press, paid every 6 months. Not payable on author-purchased copies.
  • Bookstores and individuals buying 5 or more copies get 30% off.

About the prize

The Prize celebrates the vitality, range, and possibilities of contemporary poetry. It seeks manuscripts that show artistic ambition, originality, imaginative power, technical accomplishment, and a distinctive engagement with language and experience.

It welcomes poetry that takes creative risks and offers fresh ways of encountering the world. No particular theme or school of poetry is prescribed. Beyond the win, the Prize is committed to helping excellent poetry reach readers through publication and engagement with the literary community.

Eligibility in full
  • Nigerian citizens living in Nigeria or abroad.
  • Poets of any nationality who reside in Nigeria.
  • At least 18 years old at the time of submission.
  • Poets publishing their first, second, or third full-length collection. Four or more published full-length collections makes you ineligible.
  • Chapbooks don't count towards that total; self-published full-length collections do.
  • Poems already published in journals, magazines, anthologies or online are fine.
Manuscript requirements
  • A complete manuscript not yet published as a book.
  • 45–60 poems, 60–70 pages including the title page, table of contents and any acknowledgements page.
  • A title page and table of contents.
  • An acknowledgements page listing poems published elsewhere, where applicable.
  • No author name, contact details, biography or other identifying details anywhere.
  • 12 pt Times New Roman, single-spaced, one-inch margins. Poems that need special formatting may depart from this.
What goes in your email
  • Full name, email address and telephone number.
  • Current location and full postal address.
  • A short author biography, up to 150 words.
  • A brief note of where any poems in the manuscript have previously appeared.
Simultaneous submissions

Permitted. If your manuscript is accepted elsewhere while under consideration, notify the Prize promptly and withdraw it by email.

Judging
  • Readers at Felis Catus Press read all eligible entries and forward a longlist to the judges.
  • The judges select the finalists and the winner.
  • Only the finalist(s) and winner are announced publicly. The longlist is not published.
  • Inaugural judges: names and biographies to be announced.
Governing Board

A Governing Board supports the Prize's administration, sustainability and long-term development, and provides oversight to protect its integrity. Selecting the winner remains the judges' responsibility. The inaugural Board will be announced here.

Sponsor or partner with the Prize.

We welcome individuals, foundations, cultural organisations, educational institutions and responsible businesses committed to Nigerian poetry and reading culture.

Sponsors and partners will be acknowledged here as partnerships are confirmed. This is the inaugural edition, so there are no past winners yet.

Yahuza philosophizes, he grieves, he wishes, he sings like any mourning person would a dirge.

Michael Imossan, winner of the Sillerman First Book Prize for African Poetry, 2024

Because grieving is another form of love, Adamu Yahuza's The Rainbow is Not as Beautiful as My Ruins serves as an homage to his late brother, written in profound language.

Zaynab Iliyasu Bobi, author of Cadaver of Red Roses (Derricotte/Eady Chapbook Prize 2024)

With tender ferocity, Abdullahi navigates the abyss left by the departure of a beloved brother, laying bare the brutal beauty of bereavement. These verses make the reader tremble in vulnerability.

Bà Sabouke, author of Meccan Spring

There is something so personal in "the rainbow is not as beautiful as my ruins" that feels like the poet is narrating our own experiences. The poet finds poetic ways to express his grief, telling the story of loss in a language that resonates with us all, and in clear, crisp images.

Taofeek Ayeyemi, author of Tongueless Secrets
Adamu Yahuza Abdullahi

Adamu Yahuza Abdullahi, THE PLOB, TPC V, whose works have appeared in Lolwe, Strange Horizons, Chestnut Review, A Long House, Rough Cut Press, The Temz Review, and other places, is a poet from Borgu, Nigeria. He is a pioneer resident of Muktar Aliyu Art Residency, Minna, Niger State. He is a finalist of the 2024 Garden Party Collective chapbook contest, a Best of the Net nominee, and second-place winner of both the first edition of the Hassan Sulaiman Gimba Esq Poetry Prize and the Bill Ward Poetry Prize for Emerging Writers 2023. He is a graduate of Usmanu Danfodiyo University, Sokoto.

Children of the Soil

by Wisdom Adediji

Wisdom's images stun, deject, and (when he wills) inspire hope. Most importantly, these poems pass the litmus test of Dickinson's famed saying "If I feel physically as if the top of my head were taken off, I know that is poetry." Reading these poems, I felt as such over and over. So will you.

Pamilerin Jacob, author of Cordial with Disillusionment

Wisdom's debut wears the earthiness of time and intent. This brilliant collection is a cursive junction where the self's struggles meet the shared ones, the past flows into the present, the child questioning the man he became.

Olumide Manuel, author of Hope Monger

One thing about "Children of the Soil" is its unique voice. It tells the story of different tribes of a nation, united by experience: the witnesses of slavery, both from outside and within. The manuscript takes us back in time, and leads us to the future through the unsteady present.

Taofeek Ayeyemi, author of Tongueless Secrets
Wisdom Adediji

Wisdom Adediji, NGP XI, is a Nigerian genre-bending writer. His works have appeared on Icefloe Press, Blue Press, NND Poetry Column and elsewhere. He is a poetry editor at Candid Review. He currently studies geography at the University of Ibadan and writes from there.
